**The Octagon Earthworks in Newark, Ohio: two geometric enclosures, a large circle and a larger octagon, connected by an avenue, built by the Hopewell culture between 1 and 400 CE.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Culture | Hopewell |
| Period | 1 to 400 CE |
| Form | Large octagon enclosure + circle enclosure, joined by an avenue; Observatory Mound at the circle's gateway |
| Octagon area | 50 acres (documented) |
| Circle area | 20 acres (documented) |
| Ground now | Former golf course (1910 to 2025); open to the public since 1 January 2025 |
| Designation | UNESCO World Heritage (Hopewell Ceremonial Earthworks, 2023); National Historic Landmark |
| Steward | Ohio History Connection |

**True colour** shows the site as a large green open space within a suburban grid, with old fairway lines still faintly visible. **The 1 m LiDAR** reveals what the satellite cannot: the eight straight walls and corner bastions of the octagon, the attached circle, the Observatory Mound at the circle's gateway, and the parallel-walled avenue linking the two enclosures, all rising clearly above the surrounding ground.

The Octagon Earthworks enclose 50 acres in the octagon and 20 acres in the circle, as documented, and are already on the UNESCO World Heritage list as part of the Hopewell Ceremonial Earthworks.
