**The Octagon Earthworks in Newark, Ohio: two geometric enclosures, a large circle and a larger octagon, connected by an avenue, built by the Hopewell culture between 1 and 400 CE.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Culture | Hopewell |
| Period | 1 to 400 CE |
| Form | Octagon enclosure + circle enclosure, connected by an avenue; Observatory Mound at the circle's gateway |
| Octagon area | 50 acres (documented) |
| Circle area | 20 acres (documented) |
| Designation | UNESCO World Heritage (2023); National Historic Landmark |
| Steward | Ohio History Connection |
| Ground now | Open to the public since 1 January 2025; former golf course fairways still visible from space |

**From space:** a green open area in suburban Newark, the old fairways still showing as lighter grass strips.
**In the LiDAR:** the eight walls and corner bastions of the octagon, the attached circle, the Observatory Mound at the circle's gateway, and the parallel-walled avenue all emerge clearly from the 1 m bare-earth model, invisible in true colour.

The Octagon Earthworks enclose 50 acres in the octagon and 20 acres in the circle, built by the Hopewell culture between 1 and 400 CE, and designated a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2023.
