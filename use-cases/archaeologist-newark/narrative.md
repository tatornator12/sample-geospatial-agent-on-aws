**The Octagon Earthworks in Newark, Ohio: two geometric enclosures, a large octagon and a circle, connected by an avenue, built by the Hopewell culture between 1 and 400 CE.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Culture | Hopewell |
| Period | 1 to 400 CE |
| Form | Large octagon + circle connected by an avenue; Observatory Mound at the circle's former gateway |
| Octagon enclosed area | 50 acres (documented) |
| Circle enclosed area | 20 acres (documented) |
| Ground cover | Former golf course (1910 to 2025); open to the public since January 1, 2025 |
| Designation | UNESCO World Heritage (Hopewell Ceremonial Earthworks, 2023); National Historic Landmark |

**What the views show:** From space, the site is a green open area in suburban Newark, with old fairway patterns still faintly visible. The 1 m LiDAR strips away the golf course and the suburb entirely, drawing the octagon's eight walls and corner bastions, the full circle with Observatory Mound, and the avenue connecting them, all in sharp relief.

The Octagon Earthworks in Newark, Ohio, shows two geometric enclosures, a large octagon and a circle joined by an avenue, built by the Hopewell culture between 1 and 400 CE. It is already on the World Heritage list.
